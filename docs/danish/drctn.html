§1. 

Section - Directions

§2. Translations. Translates parts of Section 4 - Directions in Standard Rules by Graham Nelson.

Direction translates into Danish as retning.

North translates into Danish as nord.
Northeast translates into Danish as nordøst.
Northwest translates into Danish as nordvest.
South translates into Danish as syd.
Southeast translates into Danish as sydøst.
Southwest translates into Danish as sydvest.
East translates into Danish as øst.
West translates into Danish as vest.
Up translates into Danish as op.
Down translates into Danish as ned.
Inside translates into Danish as indenfor.
Outside translates into Danish as udenfor.

§3. Abbreviations. In addition to these new, Danish abbreviations, we keep the English direction names and abbreviations for convenience/muscle memory, except for "se" which now means "look" (see Command Grammar).

Understand "nø" as northeast.
Understand "nv" as northwest.
Understand "sø" as southeast.
Understand "sv" as southwest.
Understand "ø" as east.
Understand "v" as west.
Understand "o" or "opad" as up.
Understand "nedad" as down.
Understand "ind" or "indeni" as inside.
Understand "ud" as outside.

§4. Articles. The English directions all have the indefinite article "the" (which, of course, is a definite article, but directions should never be indefinite), in case they're used as nouns rather than adverbs, but since saying "the North" in Danish is unnatural, we unset the indefinite articles.

One might think "Norden" (literally "the North", meaning "the Nordic countries"), "Østen" ("the East", ie. the Orient), "Syden" ("the South", the southern hemisphere) or "Vesten" ("the West", meaning "the Western world") are definite forms of the directions, and common gendered ones at that, but they are actually classified as proper nouns and therefore not relevant here.

§5. Gender. It is, however, possible to say something like "the cold North" in Danish. In that case, the neuter article should be used ("det kolde nord").

The grammatical gender of a direction is usually neuter gender.

§6. Inter hooks. We also want to make some of the direction objects visible in Inter so they can be used adverbially in |LanguageDirection| in DanishLanguageKit.

The North object is accessible to Inter as "n_obj".
The South object is accessible to Inter as "s_obj".
The East object is accessible to Inter as "e_obj".
The West object is accessible to Inter as "w_obj".
The Up object is accessible to Inter as "u_obj".
The Down object is accessible to Inter as "d_obj".
